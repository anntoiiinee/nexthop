A working network is not enough: you also need to be able to **reach** it when it is broken,
to **know** what is happening before users call, and to provide the **services** everything else
depends on (names, addresses, time). This hop groups these operations topics, the ones people often
discover on the day of an incident.

## The common thread: network failures are silent

When a server goes down, everyone knows: the service stops answering, the error is explicit. When an
authentication fails, the message says so. A network problem rarely shows itself that clearly. A link
losing 2% of its frames, one of four ECMP paths failing, a tiring optic, an MTU too small on a tunnel,
asymmetric routing: nothing is "down", but the application slows, some connections fail at random, and
nobody knows where to look. The network is then blamed by default, or wrongly cleared.

This hop teaches you to make these failures **visible**: knowing what to measure, where, and how often, to
see the degradation before users do.

## What you will learn

| Chapter | What you will be able to do |
|---|---|
| **DDI: DNS, DHCP and IPAM** | Understand the three services and why they are managed together |
| **NTP: network time** | Understand strata, choose between a local (GNSS) and an external time reference, configure and check synchronization |
| **Management and out-of-band access** | Separate the management network, set up real out-of-band access and secure it |
| **SNMP, syslog and NetFlow** | Monitor device health, centralise logs and see who uses the bandwidth |
| **Telemetry with gNMI and gNMIc** | Stream device state continuously instead of polling it |
| **Prometheus and Grafana** | Store these metrics, build dashboards and alerts |

The DDI, NTP and Management chapters can be read on their own. The three monitoring chapters build on each
other: SNMP and syslog, then the telemetry that goes beyond them, then its storage and visualisation.
