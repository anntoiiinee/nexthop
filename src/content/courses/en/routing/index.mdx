In the [Fundamentals](/en/courses/fundamentals) course, we wrote every route by hand. That works for
three routers. With thirty, or as soon as a link fails, it becomes unmanageable: routers need to
**learn the network on their own** and **adapt to failures**. That is the role of dynamic routing
protocols.

## The running example: the company grows

The branch office and headquarters from the first course are now part of a larger network. Internally,
routers exchange their routes with **OSPF**. Towards the outside, headquarters is connected to **two
ISPs** with **BGP**, so the Internet never goes down.

## What you will learn

| Chapter | What you will be able to do |
|---|---|
| **1. Why dynamic routing** | Tell IGP from EGP, distance vector from link state, understand metrics and convergence |
| **2. OSPF basics** | Bring up adjacencies, read the topology database, tune costs, split into areas |
| **3. BGP basics** | Establish an eBGP session, advertise a prefix, read a BGP table |
| **4. Attributes and best path** | Explain BGP's choice, prefer one ISP for inbound and outbound traffic |
| **5. Routing policies** | Filter with prefix-lists and route-maps, use communities, never become a transit |

## Prerequisites

The [Fundamentals](/en/courses/fundamentals) course, especially **IPv4 addressing** and **static
routing**: routing table, longest prefix match, next hop and administrative distance.

Two ContainerLab labs come with the course: `ospf-basic` (four routers in a ring) and `bgp-basic` (a
company connected to two ISPs). In both, you configure the protocol yourself: the solution is given at
the end of each chapter.
</Callout>
